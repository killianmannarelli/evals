# Chapter 24: Lebanon

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Lebanon is located in Middle East. High power distance, collectivist; cosmopolitan and sectarian; trader heritage and diaspora ties drive networks.

Key business practices:
- Trilingual deals (Arabic, French, English) [1]
- Diaspora financing critical post-2019 crisis [2]
- Banking restrictions complicate currency conversion [3]

Negotiations in Lebanon reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Napoleonic heritage), Code of Commerce 1942; sectarian system shapes government counterparties; banking secrecy law 1956 partially reformed in 2022. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Lebanon, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
The 2018 USD 11B CEDRE conference pledges, negotiated in Paris with Lebanese state and donors, underscored Lebanon's reliance on diaspora-anchored negotiations. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Lebanon. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use French or English alongside Arabic | Insist only on one language |
| Engage diaspora intermediaries | Negotiate only with onshore parties |
| Verify USD-LBP capital-control terms | Assume free repatriation |
| Respect Christian-Muslim sectarian balance | Probe political affiliations crudely |
| Build hospitality-based trust | Open with hard numbers |
