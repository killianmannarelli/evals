# Chapter 66: United States

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
United States is located in North America. Low power distance, very individualist, masculine, low uncertainty avoidance; direct, contract-heavy, litigious.

Key business practices:
- Lawyers central from day one [1]
- Time-is-money pacing [2]
- Disclosures and reps & warranties extensive [3]

Negotiations in United States reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Common law (federal + 50 states); Delaware GCL prevailing for corporates; SEC, DOJ, FTC oversight; CFIUS for FDI security review. [1]

For cross-border transactions in United States, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Microsoft's 2022 USD 68.7B Activision-Blizzard acquisition required multi-jurisdiction antitrust (FTC, CMA, EU) and exemplifies US deal complexity. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of United States. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Lead with clear executive summary | Bury the ask |
| Use first names quickly | Insist on overly formal address |
| Engage CFIUS early for FDI | Ignore national-security review |
| Provide robust reps and warranties | Skim on disclosure schedules |
| Honour litigation risk in drafting | Assume disputes won't arise |
