# Chapter 19: Iran

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Iran is located in Middle East. High power distance, collectivist, ta'arof (ritual politeness) and bazaari (merchant culture) define negotiations.

Key business practices:
- Ta'arof: ritual offers and refusals [1]
- Bazaar negotiation style; multiple rounds expected [2]
- Sanctions complicate banking and contracts [3]

Negotiations in Iran reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law with Sharia (Shia) overlay; the Foreign Investment Promotion Act 2002 governs entry but sanctions limit options. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Iran, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
The 2017 USD 5B TotalEnergies-South Pars deal (later cancelled post-2018 US sanctions) showed both Iran's negotiation pace and geopolitical exposure. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Iran. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Engage in ta'arof rituals (offer/decline three times) | Take first offers at face value |
| Use Farsi greetings (salam) respectfully | Default to Arabic |
| Conduct sanctions due diligence early | Assume EU/US compliance is simple |
| Dress conservatively; women cover hair | Wear revealing attire |
| Allow extended haggling cycles | Set tight closure deadlines |
