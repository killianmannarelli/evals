# Chapter 20: Israel

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Israel is located in Middle East. Low power distance, individualist, direct (dugri); chutzpah and rapid iteration define start-up nation negotiations.

Key business practices:
- Very direct, fast-paced discussions [1]
- Hierarchy flat; juniors challenge seniors [2]
- Sabbath (Friday eve-Saturday eve) observed [3]

Negotiations in Israel reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Mixed common-law/civil heritage; Companies Law 1999, robust IP regime, ICC and IIBA arbitration popular. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Israel, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Intel's 2017 USD 15.3B Mobileye acquisition closed in months, reflecting Israel's startup-friendly M&A culture and dugri negotiation style. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Israel. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Be direct and concise | Use overly diplomatic hedging |
| Expect aggressive counter-questions | Take pushback personally |
| Verify Shabbat-aware schedules | Book Friday-night meetings |
| Engage on tech merits deeply | Sell only on price |
| Respect security check protocols | Skip El-Al style screenings |
