# Chapter 7: Indonesia

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Indonesia is located in Southeast Asia. High power distance, collectivist, relationship-driven; bapak (father figure) leadership and rukun (harmony) prevail.

Key business practices:
- Avoid public confrontation; preserve face (malu) [1]
- Decisions go through senior bapak [2]
- Negotiations move slowly with multiple social meetings [3]

Negotiations in Indonesia reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law system inherited from Dutch colonial code; mining and natural-resources sector governed by the 2009 Mining Law and its 2020 amendments. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Indonesia, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
The 2018 Freeport-McMoRan divestment to PT Inalum for USD 3.85B took two years of negotiations over the Grasberg mine, reflecting resource-nationalist law shifts. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Indonesia. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Greet eldest or highest-ranking person first | Single out junior staff |
| Accept tea or coffee when offered | Decline hospitality outright |
| Allow extended small talk before business | Push business in opening minutes |
| Use right hand for giving/receiving items | Use the left hand |
| Build personal rapport before contract talks | Send only lawyers in the first meeting |
