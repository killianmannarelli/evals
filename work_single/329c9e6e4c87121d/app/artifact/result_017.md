# Chapter 17: United Arab Emirates

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
United Arab Emirates is located in Middle East. High power distance, collectivist with cosmopolitan overlay; Islamic ethics combined with international business pragmatism.

Key business practices:
- Free zones (DIFC, JAFZA) ease foreign ownership [1]
- Emirati nationals are senior decision-makers [2]
- Multi-cultural workforce; English widely used [3]

Negotiations in United Arab Emirates reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law inherited from Egyptian/French roots, Sharia for personal status; DIFC and ADGM offer common-law commercial courts. [1]

For cross-border transactions in United Arab Emirates, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
The 2020 USD 10.1B ADNOC pipeline deal with BlackRock-KKR consortium demonstrated the UAE's appetite for sovereign-private partnership structures. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of United Arab Emirates. [3]
