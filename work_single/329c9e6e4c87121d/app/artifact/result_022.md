# Chapter 22: Egypt

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Egypt is located in Middle East. High power distance, collectivist; hospitality (karam) and personal trust drive deals; bureaucracy heavy.

Key business practices:
- Wasta (connections) opens doors [1]
- Military-linked entities active in economy [2]
- Negotiations slow with multiple sign-offs [3]

Negotiations in Egypt reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Napoleonic Code base) with Sharia influence; Investment Law 72/2017 streamlines FDI via GAFI. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Egypt, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Eni's 2015 Zohr gas field discovery (USD 16B) and subsequent partner buy-ins (Rosneft 30%, BP 10%) involved complex EGAS-led negotiations. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Egypt. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Greet with 'Assalamu Alaikum' or 'Marhaba' | Skip greetings |
| Accept tea/coffee in elaborate ceremonies | Decline hospitality |
| Allow long bureaucratic timelines | Assume Western speed |
| Use wasta introductions effectively | Cold-call senior officials |
| Respect Friday/Sunday weekly rhythms | Schedule Friday meetings |
