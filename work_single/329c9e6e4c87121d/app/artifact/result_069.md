# Chapter 69: Australia

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Australia is located in Oceania. Low power distance, very individualist, masculine; egalitarian, direct, mateship-driven.

Key business practices:
- Mining (BHP, Rio Tinto) dominant [1]
- Egalitarian culture; first names quick [2]
- Indigenous (Native Title) consultation required [3]

Negotiations in Australia reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Common law (English heritage); Corporations Act 2001; ACCC competition oversight; FIRB for foreign-investment review. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Australia, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
BHP-Billiton's 2001 USD 28B dual-listed merger established the world's largest mining company and pioneered DLC structure. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Australia. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use first names quickly | Insist on Mr/Ms |
| Engage FIRB early for FDI | Skip foreign-investment review |
| Honour Native Title consultation | Bypass indigenous engagement |
| Be direct but humorous | Be overly formal/stiff |
| Respect summer holidays (Dec-Jan) | Schedule January deals |
