# Chapter 34: Tunisia

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Tunisia is located in North Africa. High power distance, collectivist; relatively secular and European-oriented; Francophone-Arab blend.

Key business practices:
- Trade union UGTT influential in deals [1]
- EU is dominant trade partner [2]
- Olive oil, textiles, electronics key sectors [3]

Negotiations in Tunisia reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (French heritage); Investment Law 2016 streamlined incentives via TIA; CCIT arbitration. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Tunisia, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
The 2020 European Bank for Reconstruction & Development's USD 350M Tunis-Sfax rail upgrade required UGTT and ministry tripartite talks. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Tunisia. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Engage UGTT in labour-intensive deals | Skip union consultation |
| Use French and Arabic in contracts | Submit only English |
| Leverage EU association agreement | Ignore EU origin rules |
| Respect Ramadan working hours | Push full-day intensity |
| Visit Tunis personally | Negotiate exclusively remote |
