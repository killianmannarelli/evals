# Chapter 41: Belgium

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Belgium is located in Europe. Moderate power distance, individualist, high uncertainty avoidance; Flemish-Walloon cultural duality.

Key business practices:
- Trilingual: Dutch, French, German [1]
- EU institutions headquartered in Brussels [2]
- Compromise (Belgian consensus) prized [3]

Negotiations in Belgium reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Napoleonic Code 1804 with adaptations); Companies Code 2019; CEPANI arbitration. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Belgium, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
AB InBev's 2008 USD 52B Anheuser-Busch acquisition leveraged Brussels' diplomatic culture in cross-Atlantic merger talks. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Belgium. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Switch fluidly between Dutch and French | Force one language |
| Respect linguistic-region sensitivities | Make Walloon/Flemish jokes |
| Engage EU stakeholders in Brussels | Bypass EU dimension |
| Use formal titles initially | Be overly casual |
| Allow long, consensus-building processes | Push for unilateral decisions |
