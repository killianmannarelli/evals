# Chapter 58: Brazil

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Brazil is located in Latin America. High power distance, collectivist, feminine; jeitinho (creative workaround) and personalismo central.

Key business practices:
- Long meals/social bonding precede deals [1]
- CADE merger review can be slow [2]
- Regional differences (São Paulo vs Rio) [3]

Negotiations in Brazil reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Civil Code 2002); Companies Act 6.404/76; arbitration via CAM-CCBC; antitrust via CADE. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Brazil, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Ambev-Interbrew's 2004 USD 11.5B InBev merger created the world's largest brewer and demonstrated Brazilian dealmaking sophistication. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Brazil. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use Senhor/Senhora plus first name | Default to Mr/Surname |
| Build personalismo through repeated visits | Try to close in one trip |
| Engage CADE compliance early | Underestimate antitrust review |
| Allow jeitinho-style flexibility | Insist on rigid interpretations |
| Honour Carnival timing | Schedule deals during Carnival |
