# Chapter 39: Spain

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Spain is located in Europe. Moderate-high power distance, collectivist (family), high uncertainty avoidance; personalismo and confianza shape deals.

Key business practices:
- Late dinners (10pm) for social closure [1]
- Regional autonomy (Catalonia, Basque) matters [2]
- Siesta tradition fading but lunch breaks long [3]

Negotiations in Spain reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Código Civil 1889); Companies Act 2010; arbitration via CIMA; strong consumer protection. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Spain, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
BBVA's 2020 USD 11.6B sale of US subsidiary to PNC was negotiated remotely during COVID, showing modernised Spanish cross-border M&A. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Spain. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Adopt 9-10pm dinners for relationship-building | Push 6pm dinners |
| Use Señor/Señora plus surname | Default to first names |
| Respect regional identities | Conflate all Spaniards |
| Build confianza over multiple visits | Try to close on first trip |
| Honour August holidays | Plan launches in August |
