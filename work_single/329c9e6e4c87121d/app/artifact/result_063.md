# Chapter 63: Peru

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Peru is located in Latin America. High power distance, collectivist, moderately masculine, high uncertainty avoidance; Andean indigenous-mestizo blend.

Key business practices:
- Mining (copper, zinc) anchors economy [1]
- Lima dominant but Andean stakeholder consultation vital [2]
- Political instability affects continuity [3]

Negotiations in Peru reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Civil Code 1984); General Corporations Law 1997; arbitration via CCL Lima; mining-sector central. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Peru, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
China Minmetals's 2014 USD 7B Las Bambas copper acquisition required years of community-consultation talks in Apurímac. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Peru. [3]
