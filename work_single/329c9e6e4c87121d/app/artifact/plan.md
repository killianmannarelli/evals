# Cross-Cultural Negotiation Handbook — Master Plan

## Description

This document is the master blueprint for the creation of a comprehensive handbook on cross-cultural negotiation. The handbook contains one detailed chapter per country, drawn from a roster of 80 countries spanning all inhabited continents. Each chapter is written to a common template to guarantee structural consistency across the final 250-page handbook. The chapters are intended to be a practical resource for international business executives, diplomats, lawyers, and academics who must negotiate across borders.

## Output Schema (Chapter Template)

Each chapter MUST follow this template precisely. All factual claims should carry a footnote marker (e.g., `[1]`) and a corresponding entry in the References section.

```
STATUS: complete

# Chapter N. {Country Name}

## 1. Cultural Context

Concise summary of the cultural dimensions that shape negotiations: communication style (high vs. low context), relationship orientation, time orientation, hierarchy/power distance, individualism vs. collectivism, and decision-making norms.[#]

## 2. Legal Framework

Overview of the legal system family (civil law, common law, religious, mixed), contract law features, enforceability of MOUs, dispute resolution preferences (litigation vs. arbitration), and any unique statutory quirks travelers must know.[#]

## 3. Real-World Case Study

A verifiable negotiation case study from the past 30 years. State the parties, year, sector, headline issue, negotiation arc (positions, BATNAs, deadlocks, breakthroughs), and outcome. Extract 2-3 transferable lessons.[#]

## 4. Do / Don't Table

```

## Quality Criteria

A chapter is **complete** when:
*   All five sections are present with substantive prose (cultural context >=120 words, legal framework >=100 words, case study >=140 words).
*   At least 3 distinct, named footnotes/citations appear in the body and are listed in the References section.
*   A verifiable, real-world case study is identified by year, parties, and outcome.
*   The do/don't table has at least 5 rows.

A chapter is **partial** when:
*   One or more sections are missing, stubbed, or have fewer than the minimum words.
*   Fewer than 3 citations are provided, or the references list is incomplete.
*   The case study is generic or unverifiable.

## Workflow

1.  Phase 1: Create this plan.
2.  Phase 2: Launch 80 parallel sub-agents, each researching and writing one chapter.
3.  Phase 3: Audit STATUS lines; relaunch failed/partial chapters.
4.  Phase 4: Assemble alphabetized handbook with table of contents, intro, and summary statistics.
