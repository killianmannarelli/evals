# Chapter 35: Germany

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Germany is located in Europe. Low power distance, individualist, high uncertainty avoidance; punctuality, precision, and process-orientation dominate.

Key business practices:
- Detailed agendas and dossiers expected [1]
- Decisions follow technical evaluation [2]
- Works councils consulted in M&A [3]

Negotiations in Germany reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (BGB 1900); strong codetermination via Mitbestimmung (works councils); DIS arbitration; strict GDPR/IP. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Germany, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Linde-Praxair's 2018 USD 90B industrial-gas merger required two years of EU/US/Chinese antitrust talks plus Mitbestimmung negotiations. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Germany. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Arrive 5-10 minutes early | Show up late |
| Provide technical data upfront | Lead with marketing fluff |
| Use formal Sie pronoun and titles | Switch to du prematurely |
| Involve Betriebsrat in workforce decisions | Bypass works councils |
| Stick to agenda | Detour into unrelated topics |
