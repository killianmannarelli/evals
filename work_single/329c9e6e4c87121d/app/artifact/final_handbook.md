# Chapter 33: Algeria

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Algeria is located in North Africa. High power distance, collectivist; bureaucratic, oil-dependent; French language secondary to Arabic.

Key business practices:
- Sonatrach dominates oil/gas [1]
- Bureaucracy heavy, payments slow [2]
- French and Arabic both used [3]

Negotiations in Algeria reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (French heritage); Hydrocarbons Law 2019 reopened upstream; Investment Law 2022 lifted 51-49 rule in many sectors. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Algeria, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
Eni's 2022 USD 4B Berkine gas deal with Sonatrach showcased post-reform Algerian openness after years of restrictive 51-49 ownership rules. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Algeria. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Engage Sonatrach senior leadership | Bypass national oil company |
| File documents in Arabic and French | Submit English-only documents |
| Allow long payment cycles | Expect rapid disbursements |
| Use new investment-law incentives | Assume old 51-49 still applies |
| Respect Ramadan and Friday rhythms | Force Western calendar |

## References
[1] AAPI Algeria, 'Investment Guide 2022.'
[2] Hofstede Insights: Algeria (regional est.).
[3] Aïssaoui, A., 'Algeria's Oil and Gas,' OIES, 2016.
