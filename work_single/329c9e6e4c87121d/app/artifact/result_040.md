# Chapter 40: Netherlands

STATUS: complete

## Cultural Context
Netherlands is located in Europe. Very low power distance, individualist, feminine; egalitarian and very direct (bespreekbaarheid).

Key business practices:
- Polder model: consensus among stakeholders [1]
- Flat hierarchy; junior staff speak up [2]
- Direct, even blunt feedback common [3]

Negotiations in Netherlands reward parties that combine technical preparation with cultural fluency. Locals expect counterparts to understand both the codified rules and the unwritten conventions of relationships, hospitality, and hierarchy that shape every meeting.

## Legal Framework
Civil law (Burgerlijk Wetboek 1992); Dutch Corporate Governance Code; NAI arbitration; works councils via WOR. [1]

For cross-border transactions in Netherlands, parties should pay attention to:
- Choice-of-law and dispute-resolution clauses (arbitration vs courts).
- Sector-specific licensing and screening requirements (e.g., foreign-investment review, antitrust, sanctions/export controls).
- Tax, transfer-pricing, and withholding obligations under local statute and applicable treaties. [2]

## Case Study
ASML's 2016 USD 3.1B Hermes Microvision acquisition and ongoing Dutch-US export-control talks highlight precision and transparency in Dutch deals. The episode is widely cited as a benchmark for how foreign and domestic parties balance commercial ambition with the cultural and institutional realities of Netherlands. [3]

## Do's & Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Welcome direct critique | Take frankness personally |
| Use first names quickly | Insist on formal Mr/Mrs |
| Apply polder consensus building | Push top-down decisions |
| Respect cycling-based punctuality | Run 30+ minutes late |
| Honour OR/works-council rights | Bypass employee voice |
